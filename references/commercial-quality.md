# Commercial-quality standard

## 1. Product truth

- Trace every feature, state, icon, label, and claim to the requested source version or user confirmation.
- Use real component geometry and visual tokens where available.
- Art direction may transform framing, lighting, depth, and composition, but must remain recognizably related to the product.
- Do not fabricate dense feature grids from generic cards when the source does not support them.

## 2. Art direction

- Establish one visual system: palette, surface language, border logic, shadows, glow, texture, and depth.
- Use negative space intentionally, not as leftover emptiness.
- Avoid filling every area; balance mass, rhythm, and breathing room.
- Make the hero area unmistakable while supporting panels remain subordinate.
- Prefer a few strong, source-backed modules over many weak tiles.

## 3. Typography and copy

- Forbid meaningless small text used only as decoration.
- Use at most three functional type levels.
- Keep primary claims readable on the smallest intended screen.
- Define every headline as `single-line` or with exact intentional line breaks.
- Do not let responsive layout create an unplanned second line.
- Use `white-space: nowrap` only for content designed and tested to fit; otherwise author exact line containers.
- Detect clipping, overflow, widows, inconsistent font sizes, and low contrast.
- Enter quickly enough to leave real reading time; do not spend most of a scene assembling the title.

## 4. Motion and camera

- Center the meaningful group, not the nearest button.
- Use one decisive focus move per action phase.
- Keep arrival stable while subtle camera drift, depth, highlight, or parallax maintains life.
- Avoid incremental corrections that make the same subject change position three or four times.
- Keep transitions causal and inherit at least one property from the previous state.
- Separate beat-synced element accents from scene duration; a scene can breathe while its internal elements hit the beat.

## 5. Launch-summary composition

For a final release-summary or keynote page:

- derive all modules from real v/source-version product features and UI states;
- place the real product logo/name and a short release statement in the hero region;
- feature 3–5 primary capabilities, not a wall of equally loud claims;
- give each module one concise readable statement and a truthful UI fragment;
- avoid both extremes: cramped giant type and empty panels with missing product identity;
- assemble quickly, then hold long enough to scan;
- keep intentional line policies and alignment across modules;
- transition to the slogan and official URL with a clear release, not an abrupt disappearance.

## 6. Readability budget

- Allocate entrance, unobstructed reading, and exit separately.
- Do not count blurred or moving entry frames as reading time.
- If copy cannot be read at normal playback, enlarge it, shorten it, extend the scene, or intentionally blur it as context.
- Keep the important content sharp while irrelevant copy may dim or defocus.
- Test the intended phone/small-screen viewing condition.

## 7. Commercial-ready decision

Block a commercial-ready claim unless all are true:

- source fidelity is complete;
- story and primary promise are unambiguous;
- every interaction is spatially correct;
- all important text is readable and intentionally broken;
- music climax supports the main value;
- every scene has purposeful continuous motion;
- transitions are visually and narratively inherited;
- the browser preview received explicit approval;
- final export passes decode, continuity, dimension, frame-rate, audio, and visual checks.
